Part 01 Pricing Page Audit

stripe.com/pricing  ·  Clarity vs. Conversion Audit

Observed gaps in how the page earns trust at key decision moments

What's there now

"Integrated per-transaction fees, no hidden costs." The page leads with fees, not outcomes. The reader's first question after landing is "how much does this cost?" rather than "what does this actually do for my business?"

Where it could go

Flip the frame: lead with what the business keeps, not what Stripe charges. "You set the price. We handle everything between the customer's card and your bank account." That's a value position, not a pricing position.

Why it matters to the PMM Pricing role: Pricing pages that lead with the fee structure anchor the conversation on cost before the user understands value. Stripe's product suite is genuinely deep, but the page sells at the surface. The opportunity is to write pricing copy that builds confidence before revealing the number, not after.
What's there now

Payments, Billing, Terminal, Radar, and others are listed with their own pricing sections, but there's no visible story connecting them. A prospect evaluating three Stripe products has to do their own math to understand the bundle value.

Where it could go

A portfolio view layer that lets users build their own stack and see what the combined cost looks like, alongside a plain-English breakdown of what each product does when they work together. Price visibility plus product clarity, in one place.

The structural gap: Right now the page is organized by product, not by business need. Most companies don't buy "Payments + Radar + Billing" as individual decisions. They buy "I need to start taking money, stay compliant, and automate invoicing." Organizing the page around those decisions instead of the product names would reduce cognitive load significantly.
What's there now

"Custom pricing available. Contact sales." This is the most load-bearing sentence on the page for enterprise deals, and it does almost nothing. It doesn't tell the reader what signals a custom deal, who to expect on the call, or what they'll walk away with.

Where it could go

"Processing over $1M a year? Our pricing team builds a model around your volume, payment mix, and geography. Most enterprise conversations take about two calls. Here's what to bring." That's still short, but it converts curiosity into intent.

The enterprise trust problem: Large buyers need to see that Stripe understands their complexity before they pick up the phone. "Contact sales" says nothing about whether Stripe has done this before for a business like theirs. Adding one sentence of specificity, volume threshold, team composition, timeline, turns a dead-end into a soft commitment.
Part 02 GTM Brief: Pricing Narrative Launch

Portfolio Pricing GTM Brief

How to take Stripe's multi-product pricing story from internal clarity to external conviction

Cross-functional

The Opportunity

Stripe has a pricing comprehension problem, not a pricing problem. The product is competitively priced. But when a prospect tries to figure out what Stripe will actually cost them, specifically for their volume, their products, their geography, there's too much friction between curiosity and clarity.

The PMM opportunity is to own how Stripe talks about pricing across every surface: the public page, sales enablement materials, competitive plays, and product-led onboarding. That's a narrative job as much as a strategy job.

What "Winning" Looks Like

A prospect in a competitive deal should be able to explain Stripe's pricing model to their CFO without needing a Stripe sales rep in the room. That's the bar. If they can't, the pricing story isn't working hard enough.

Conversion: More prospects completing the "calculate your cost" flow before contacting sales.
Sales velocity: Fewer pricing objections as a blocker in mid-market deals.
Attach rate: More single-product customers expanding to two or more products within 90 days.

Target Signals by Segment

Startups (self-serve): First payment processed, looking to add Billing or Radar. Needs to understand whether adding products increases complexity or reduces it.

Mid-market (sales-assisted): Evaluating Stripe against Adyen or Braintree. Pricing objections come up early. Sales needs a clear value-per-dollar story, not just a features list.

Enterprise: Multi-geography, multiple payment methods, needs to see that Stripe's model scales predictably and that someone will actually model it for them.

Core Narrative Principles

Lead with the cost of not having it. Stripe's fraud tools, for example, are often framed as an add-on cost. The real frame is: what is a chargeback rate of 0.8% costing you right now?

Make the bundle legible. Multi-product customers need to see the combined value, not five separate line items. Pricing pages that feel like menus lose to pricing pages that feel like a recommendation.

Respect the CFO in the room. Enterprise buyers will have a finance person review any deal over a threshold. The pricing materials need to hold up in that room without a Stripe rep explaining them.

30-60-90 Day Plan

Days 1-30
Listen and map. Shadow five to eight sales calls. Interview the pricing team, finance, and two or three current enterprise customers who evaluated competitors. Document where the current narrative breaks down and at what point in the buying process.
Days 31-60
Build the asset layer. Rewrite the pricing page messaging for one segment (mid-market as a starting point, most acute need). Draft a competitive pricing playbook for the sales team covering the top three objections against Adyen and PayPal. Run one pricing experiment through the Growth Product team on the calculator flow.
Days 61-90
Close the loop. Measure pricing objection rate in deals before and after the playbook. Review attach rate for the segment targeted. Present a portfolio narrative framework to the PMM team for feedback and iterate toward a full pricing page refresh brief.

Part 04 Copy Rewrites

Three real Stripe surfaces where the pricing message could work harder. Each one includes the reasoning, not just the rewrite, because the thinking matters as much as the words.

Pricing Page  ·  Hero Web
One rate. Every card. No surprises when you reconcile.
Stripe charges 2.9% + 30 cents per successful card transaction. That's it. No monthly fees, no setup, no minimum. If a payment doesn't go through, you don't pay. Add Billing, Radar, or any other Stripe product when you need it. Your rate doesn't change because you grew.
Why it works: The current hero is accurate but passive. This version answers the three questions a founder actually has when they land on the pricing page: what does it cost, are there hidden fees, and will the price go up if I succeed. It names specific products without forcing the reader to go look for them, and ends with a commitment that removes a real anxiety about growing out of a pricing tier.
Sales Email  ·  Competitive Re-engagement Email
You already know what Adyen costs. Here's what it doesn't include.
Happy to send over a line-by-line comparison, but honestly the more useful conversation is around total cost. Adyen charges lower per-transaction rates in some regions. What that number doesn't capture is what you spend on implementation, the engineering time on custom fraud rules, and the reconciliation overhead when you're operating across three currencies. We've modelled this for companies similar to yours. Worth 20 minutes?
Why it works: Sales emails that lead with "here's why we're better" lose to sales emails that acknowledge the real objection and reframe it. This one validates the prospect's research, then expands the frame from per-transaction rate to total cost of ownership, which is where Stripe tends to win against Adyen for mid-market companies. The question at the end is specific enough to lower friction without being pushy.
Sales Enablement  ·  Pricing Objection Card Enablement
When they say "your rate is higher than [competitor]"
Don't defend the rate. Validate the observation, then expand the equation. "You're right, and that's worth talking through. The per-transaction number is real. What most companies find when they do the full model is that the rate is one variable in a bigger equation that includes fraud losses, implementation cost, and how much engineering time you want spending on payments infrastructure versus your core product. Can I share how we've built that model for a company in a similar position to yours?" Then send the comparison doc.
Why it works: The instinct when a prospect raises a price objection is to defend or discount. Neither builds confidence. This card tells the rep to agree with the observation, which disarms the objection, and then shift the conversation to total cost. It also gives the rep a concrete next step so the conversation doesn't stall. The best pricing enablement doesn't just give reps talking points. It gives them a sequence.
